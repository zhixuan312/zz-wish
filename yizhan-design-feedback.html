Give the wish in your heart
one small light.

One wish, one candle you pick or draw yourself, and a stretch of time you choose to give it. You go study or work; the candle burns quietly in front of you. Burning down leaves a complete trace; going out leaves an honest one.

A long-form focus experience.

Setup and ending are short; a candle can burn for up to 24 hours. The pull comes from your own wish, your own candle, and a lighting that cannot be repeated.

Rev 2 applies the owner's feedback of 2026-11-16. It is a specification; the prototype does not implement it yet.

01
Hands-on run of index.html

What the prototype does today

The prototype was driven once in desktop Chrome with GPU rendering (Apple M5, ANGLE Metal) along one path: wish → place → travel → drawing a candle → offering → lighting → hiding the tab. That path works. Not run: picking and lighting a preset, a full burn-down, manual put-out, the butter lamp, a phone-size flow, Safari. Rows marked "Source" were read from the code, not observed. Its strongest parts stay: one decision per screen, a flame in a dark hall, wish before journey, and the camera closing in on your own candle. What it lacks is the product around the candle: a real record, real presence and rules that hold.

Smoothness, measured

60 fpsAll phases, including burning with 4x CPU slowdown. This is the display's vsync cap on an M5 and says nothing about phones.

02
Short setup, long company, light ending

The full story: one candle for one wish

No protagonist, quest system or pop-up plot. The person is the story: why they light it, where, what their candle looks like, and what trace it leaves.

Arrive: know what to do in one line

Give what's on your mind one small light.

Land directly on the wish input. A quiet top bar holds the zh / en switch, "Ten Lights" (public stats) and "My Candles". The first visit shows the rules in two lines. No sign-up, no avatar.

Wish: tie this candle to your own matter

What is this light for?

Up to 60 characters, with a live counter. No categories. The wish is private: never shown publicly, never in public stats, never in logs.

Primary button: "Set my wish". Going back keeps the text and every later choice.

Choose a place: where this time is spent

Where would you like to leave it?

Ten places in one fixed order. Each card shows the name, one line of atmosphere and the live count of people wishing there now. The person decides; nothing is recommended.

Primary button: "Leave it here". A place is an atmosphere, not a success rate.

Pick or draw: choose the time you will give

Pick a candle, or draw your own.

"Pick one" shows the eight presets, time first. "Draw" shows the pad; the duration updates as the shape changes, from 5 minutes up to 24 hours. Colour and decoration never change time.

Primary button: "Take this one · 2 h 15 min". No hour or minute input box.

Arrive and place it: short, with a sense of arrival

It's in place, waiting for you.

First journey 6–8 s, return visits about 2 s, skippable at once. Before lighting, confirm wish, place, candle, duration and the one-lighting rule, and request screen wake lock.

Light button: "Light it · 2 h 15 min". Sound starts only after this tap; quiet by default.

Burning: you work, it stays here

Keep this page in front. Go do your own thing.

The flame burns and the wax drops in real time. A small presence pill reads "You and 11 others are wishing here now". No check-ins, no hold-to-keep, no tasks.

Not moving the mouse, reading or writing never counts as leaving. Leaving the screen does.

Burned down: fulfilment told by the scene

The flame shrinks, the last thread of smoke clears, a complete wax trace stays, and the place brightens slightly. At most one soft sound, which can be muted.

This light has burned down.

The record states facts: "Kept you company for the full 2 h 15 min". Never "wish fulfilled".

Went out: this time stops here

This light stops here.

The candle keeps its half-burned shape; the wish, the actual burn time and the reason enter the record. It cannot be lit again.

Primary: "See this light". Secondary: "Light a new one for this wish", which copies the wish and starts a new candle with its own duration and confirmation.

Completion comes from "I really set aside this time for this wish."

Burning down has a fulfilled afterglow; going out leaves an honest trace. Never explain an interruption with "not sincere enough" or guilt copy.

Choosing a candle is choosing a stretch of time

Presets for speed, freehand for any length up to 24 hours

Two ways in, one rule underneath: time is proportional to the wax you see. Presets are simply candles drawn for you at common lengths.

Presets: time at a glance

Desktop 4 x 2, phone 2 x 4. Each card shows the candle shape and the time in large type. 24 hours is not a higher rank or a stronger wish.

Draw: you draw a candle, you get its time

  1. Draw as in the prototype. Strokes on the 10 x 15 cm pad become the candle's outline row by row (the prototype's stroke-to-outline method is the rule). Long, short, thick, thin, lopsided. Undo and redraw. Recommendation: offer a default outline to start from.
  2. The duration follows the shape live. Rounded to the minute, shown as "2 h 15 min". While drawing, the readout updates at most a few times per second so it does not flicker.
  3. Limits are visible. Below 5 minutes the readout shows the real value and "Draw a little more wax: at least 5 min", and "Take this one" stays disabled. At the pad's full size: "This is the 24-hour maximum".
  4. Colour and decoration never change time. Re-drawing the same line adds nothing; only the filled outline counts.
  5. Confirm, then lock. "Take this one · 2 h 15 min". At lighting, the outline, duration and wish snapshot lock.
  6. Keep the handmade look. The scene and the record show the person's own shape, not a standard cylinder.

Try it: shape to time

A rule demo, not the drawing editor. Height and width are in pad centimetres (the pad is 10 x 15 cm). The result is continuous between 5 minutes and 24 hours.

For implementers: the one duration formula
pad = 10 cm wide x 15 cm tall (as in the prototype)
outline = filled outline, sampled as rows (height dy, radius r_i = half the row width)
waxVolume  = sum(pi * r_i^2 * dy)                    // every bit of wax counts, no 8 cm cap
fullVolume = pi * 5^2 * 15 = 1178.1 cm^3             // a cylinder filling the whole pad
rawMinutes = 1440 * waxVolume / fullVolume
minutes    = min(round(rawMinutes), 1440)
allowed    = minutes >= 5                            // below 5: button disabled, server rejects
store: duration_seconds = minutes * 60, shape (outline rows), rule_version
Client preview and server validation run the same function and rule_version.

Presets are stored shapes whose formula result equals their label (10 min, 30 min, ...), so presets and freehand obey one rule. A typical 2 x 10.8 cm stroke gives about 41 min, close to the prototype's 38 min. Constraints: one connected, hole-free outline that stands on the holder; small gaps auto-close. Smoothing may change the look, never the confirmed time.

"Total" and "remaining" are different fields.

The total is fixed at lighting (any whole minute from 5 to 1440). The remaining time counts down continuously. UI and data keep them apart.

The butter lamp at Jokhang

Jokhang keeps its butter lamp. The same rule applies to the lamp's fill: presets are the eight durations; "Fill your own" lets the person drag the butter level in the cup, and the filled volume maps linearly to 5 minutes up to 24 hours (a full cup = 24 h). The lamp's pattern and colour are looks only. This is a content adaptation, not a claim about real ritual.

05
One lighting, no second chance

"Not on screen" means out, defined case by case

The rule carries the product's weight and is stated before lighting. After lighting there is no pause, resume, relight or undo. The person can still choose to end; they get a record that cannot be revived.

What "reasonable" means

  • The product never causes its own extinguish. Permission prompts (wake lock, sound) happen before lighting. No modal while burning, OS notification or redirect that takes focus. A deploy never force-reloads a page with a burning candle.
  • Focus moves inside the page do not count. Only page-level signals do: visibility hidden, window blur, pagehide, and in-app navigation.
  • Settle first, animate second. On any signal, take the end time, compute burned time, stop locally and queue the record; then play the smoke.
  • Unknown is not guilty. Confirmed causes are written as "left the page" or "put out by you"; unconfirmed ones as "ended unexpectedly".
How long sessions actually work.

Best: a phone left on the burning screen while the person reads, writes, or works on another device. On a computer, clicking into a document or IDE in another window puts the candle out. Say this honestly in the rules; do not promise same-device multitasking.

Visibility events see tab switches and hiding; window.blur is a window-focus signal and can also fire for browser UI such as the address bar. Using blur follows the owner's strict direction; it is not attention detection. [W1]

Anonymous unique ID: a refresh is still you

  1. First visit: the server creates an anonymous identity and sets a first-party credential. No sign-up.
  2. Later visits in the same browser: the server finds that person's records in SQLite.
  3. A candle burning at refresh time ends; reload only restores history.
  4. Each person reads only their own wishes and shapes; public endpoints return aggregates only.

Credential: a server-issued random opaque cookie, HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, long expiry, mapped server-side to an internal visitor ID. Clearing site data, another browser or another device is a new anonymous visitor; say so in the help. Recovery codes and export come later. [W11]

Presence: delay allowed, faking never

The place, candle and burning screens hold one WebSocket. Before lighting it only receives the place's count; with a burning candle it also sends a heartbeat every 10 s and is counted. A visitor drops from "now" after 30 s without a heartbeat (worst case about 40 s), or at once on a clean close. Offline: "Live count unavailable" or the last value with its time, never a fake 0. A missed heartbeat only means presence is unconfirmed; it is not proof the candle went out. These are proposed parameters, not measured performance.

Decoration candles in the scene stay decoration. If other people's flames are drawn, they come from live data and aggregate when there are many; the 66 rack candles are never presented as 66 people.

10
Two languages, honest copy, kind to long sessions

zh / en, copy, and long-session care

Bilingual

  • Scope: the whole product. Every screen, message, place name, status and error exists in Chinese and English.
  • Switch on the home screen (a two-option toggle in the top bar, each option labelled in its own script), also reachable from My Candles and Ten Lights. Not shown while a candle burns.
  • Follows the visitor's locale on first visit (navigator.languages and Accept-Language: zh* → Chinese, otherwise English). A manual choice is remembered per visitor (cookie plus server profile) and wins from then on. <html lang>, dates, times and numbers follow the active language through Intl.
  • Wishes are not translated. A wish stays in the language it was written in.
  • Typed locale dictionaries (zh, en) with a build check that every key exists in both. Place names, atmosphere lines, statuses and all errors are keys.
  • Fonts: a Songti-style serif for zh titles and a matching serif for en titles; system sans for buttons, help and numbers.

24 hours without showing off

  • Render clock and burn clock are separate. Power saver lowers resolution and frame rate and trims bloom, particles and camera drift; time stays exact.
  • Wake lock status is real. Request Screen Wake Lock before lighting; say "Screen kept on" only on success, explain when it fails or is released. [W3]
  • Power saver never bends the rule. Fewer pixels, yes; burning in the background, no.
  • Quiet by default. Collapsible wish and countdown; sound off or soft; Put out always findable.
  • Across midnight. Duration from lighting; records show full start and end dates in local time.
  • Deploys never reload a burning page. Update prompts wait until the candle ends.

Deploying outside mainland China
  • HTTPS is required. Screen Wake Lock only works in a secure context, the identity cookie is Secure behind HTTPS, and presence uses wss://. Terminate TLS at the host or a reverse proxy (Caddy obtains certificates automatically) and forward to port 8787; the server trusts X-Forwarded-Proto.
  • Host shape: one always-on container with a persistent volume mounted at /data. Good fits: a small VPS (for example Hetzner or DigitalOcean) running Docker plus Caddy, or Fly.io with one machine and one volume, or Render or Railway with a persistent disk. Never run two instances against one database file.
  • Region: pick the region closest to most visitors (for example Singapore or Tokyo for East Asia, Frankfurt for Europe). Static assets are hashed and can be put behind a CDN later; the API and socket stay on the one server.
  • WebSocket: the proxy must pass Upgrade headers for /api/presence and allow idle connections of at least 60 s (heartbeats are every 10 s).
  • Backups: a daily VACUUM INTO copy of /data/one-light.db to object storage, plus one before every deploy that changes the schema; rehearse a restore once.
  • Deploys: a restart drops live sockets, and any candle burning at that moment becomes "ended unexpectedly" and is reconciled when its page reports; deploy at quiet hours until zero-downtime handover is built (Tier 2).
  • Fonts: the Noto Serif SC web font loads from Google Fonts, which works outside mainland China; self-hosting it is Tier 2.

Smoothness plan

Correct timing at any frame rate is Tier 1. Frame rate, load time and heat are Tier 2: measured, compared with the last release, reported, never blocking.

Implementation spec

States move one way

StateNextInvariant
DraftEdit, discard, light → burningNot in any public count; wish, place and shape editable.
BurningTime reached → burned down; leave or put out → went out; no live run when the visitor comes back → ended unexpectedlyOne burning candle per visitor; wish and duration locked.
Burned downView; copy the wish into a new candleFinal.
Went outView; copy the wish into a new candleFinal; remaining wax is history, not credit.
Ended unexpectedlyReconcile with evidence from the same runNever relit; never assumed burned down by wall clock. Set when the visitor loads any page while an old candle is still burning with no live run, so a crash never blocks the next candle.

"Saving" is a save state, not a burn state. A failed save never turns a went-out candle back into a burning one.

Data model: few entities
TableKey fieldsUse
visitorsid, credential hash, locale, created_at, last_seen_atSame person after refresh; language preference.
wishesid, visitor_id, text, created_atGroups candles for "your wishes"; private.
candlesid, visitor_id, wish_id, wish_snapshot, place_id, kind (candle / lamp), shape JSON, colour, preset_minutes (nullable), duration_seconds, rule_version, status, started_at, ended_at, burned_seconds, end_reason, run_idThe long-term record; one lighting per row.
presenceIn server memory, not a table: visitor, candle, place, last heartbeat per socketShort-lived; never history. A restart empties it.

Places are versioned static config in shared/. Public stats are queries over candles (indexed by place_id, status), not separately maintained counters, until load says otherwise.

API and server rules
  • GET /api/me: issue or reuse the cookie; return the visitor's locale and totals. Never trust a client-supplied visitor ID.
  • POST /api/candles lights a candle in one call (drafts stay in the browser): recompute duration from the shape with the shared rule; reject below 5 or above 1440 min; idempotent by request key; refuse if another candle of this visitor is burning.
  • POST /api/candles/:id/end: one final state per candle; late "light" or stale heartbeats cannot overwrite it; reason and burned seconds recorded.
  • GET /api/me/candles, GET /api/me/wishes: always filtered by the verified visitor.
  • GET /api/places/stats: aggregates only, sortable by people, time, now.
  • WS /api/presence: join with candle and run; heartbeat; server pushes the place's live count and "someone lit here" events. No wish text ever crosses it.

Transactions, unique constraints and idempotency keys cover double taps and retries; no heavy anti-abuse system in v1.

Timing and lifecycle: no fake results after 24 hours
  1. Never count frames. Progress = (now − start) from a monotonic clock; the animation only reads it. [W4]
  2. Lighting sets state first. The leave rules apply from the tap, not after the 1.6 s ignition animation.
  3. Leaving settles first. End time → burned time → stop → queue record → send (with fetch keepalive on hide, plus a local retry queue) → animate.
  4. Burn-down and leave at once resolve in one settlement: reached total → burned down, else went out.
  5. Closing is not a reliable save point. Visibility change is the main signal, pagehide a backup; light and end are sent immediately and progress is checkpointed. [W2]
  6. Reopening only reads. Refresh and back-forward cache never restore the fire.
SQLite deploy and long-term keeping
  • The database lives on a persistent volume, never in the build output or an ephemeral directory.
  • One service instance owns the file; never one app.db per instance.
  • WAL mode, short transactions, indexes; all processes on the same host, no network file system. [W9]
  • Daily backups plus one before each migration, with VACUUM INTO or the Online Backup API; a restore is rehearsed once. [W10]
  • Redeploys keep identities and history; old pages keep working until their candle ends.
