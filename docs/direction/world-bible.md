# One Light world bible

Status: approved by the owner.

## Rules for every place

1. **A real reason to exist.** Each place stands where real people would have built it and is still used in a believable way.
2. **People are felt, never seen.** The world is full of people, but this is one person's moment, so no characters appear. Signs of daily life are welcome: swept paths, footprints, smoke,
   firewood, tools, tea bowls and repairs.
3. **Ordinary lamps are allowed.** Everyday lamps light the place for practical reasons and look plainly different from a wish lamp.
4. **Wishing is an ordinary custom.** Passers-by often stop and light a wish lamp, so the visitor finds many earlier wish lamps.
   Most have burnt out and remain as stubs, cold wax or faded lanterns, and two or three may still be burning low. They belong to
   the fictional world. Online players never change what another player sees.
5. **The visitor's lamp is the hero.** One open place waits at the centre of the wish lamps. The visitor's lamp is the nearest,
   brightest and most detailed, and lighting it joins the others.
6. **Presence text counts people online, never lamps.** It must not say that the visitor's lamp is the only light here.
7. **Compression, then release.** The walk moves from enclosed to open, ending in a wide view that makes the visitor stop.
8. **No edge of the world.** Every view has foreground, middle ground and far distance.
9. **Every time of day.** Every place is designed for dawn, morning, midday, afternoon, dusk and night, following the player's
   local time with smooth changes. Each time has its own light, colour, sound and mood.
10. **Always easy to see.** At every time of day the player can clearly read the architecture, path, props and lamp. Night has a
    dark sky and a calm mood, but buildings, ground and interiors stay visible.
11. **Music from start to finish.** Music plays through the whole game, from the home page to the ending, and the player can mute it.
12. **Believable, generic faith.** Places may hold ordinary Chinese religious figures such as a Buddha, Guan Gong or a local earth
    god. Nothing may identify one specific real temple.

## Snow Roof Court 

**Place:** a small temple at a pass on an old mountain road above a northern market town, in deep winter, with snow on the ground at every time of day.

**Why it exists:** the pass is the windiest point of the crossing. A shelter was built there long ago and became a temple. Travellers
and townspeople stop here, and hang a red gauze lantern before a long journey or for someone far away.

**Scale:** one modest walled court with a gate, main hall, two side rooms and a kitchen. The hall holds a generic seated Buddha.

**Condition:** old, patched and lived in. Worn red paint, mended tiles, and one side room boarded up for winter.

**Signs of life:** a narrow swept path, footprints half covered by new snow, kitchen smoke, stacked firewood, a broom and warm paper windows.

**Ordinary lamps:** two plain oil lamps at the gate, a small altar lamp and a storm lantern on the veranda.

**Wish lamps:** one short, orderly row of six or seven red gauze lanterns with wooden tags hangs at one height from a single beam, evenly
spaced. Most are out and faded to a warm soft red or rose, never grey or white, and two still glow faintly. The cord at the centre of the
row is free for the visitor. The row must feel calm and cared for, never crowded or eerie (owner direction, 2026-11-20).

**Walk:** the snowy road through wind-bent pines and rock, the two gate lamps seen from afar, steps up through the gate, then the court
opening with a moonlit snow peak rising behind the hall.

**Burning view:** the visitor's lantern close to camera, the old lanterns behind it, and wind-blown snow glittering beyond the half-open hall door.

**Endings:** burnt down, the lantern dims slowly and becomes one more old lantern. Put out, it goes dark at once with a thread of smoke.

**Sound:** wind, soft snowfall, a distant bell and the small sound of the flame.

## Pine Gate Steps 

**Place:** a level mountain shoulder halfway up an old pilgrim road in the humid south, above a farming village.

**Why it exists:** the climb's only flat ground and spring. Villagers built a small mountain shrine and a free tea pavilion here for
porters, pilgrims and woodcutters.

**Scale:** one small shrine with a simple local earth god, one open tea pavilion, a stone table, a spring basin and two ancient pines.

**Condition:** old and mossy but looked after, with new tiles among the old.

**Signs of life:** steps worn hollow, muddy footprints, a bamboo broom, coarse tea bowls, a small stove with warm ash, a bamboo pipe feeding
the basin and a carrying pole left against a post.

**Ordinary lamps:** a storm lantern under the pavilion eave and a small oil lamp before the shrine's image.

**Wish lamps:** stone ledges around the shrine niche hold many burnt-out candle stubs over layers of old wax, with two or three short
candles still burning. Red cloth strips are tied to the old pine. The centre of the stone lamp shrine is free for the visitor.

**Walk:** a narrow stone path climbs the shaded back of a ridge under dense pines. Two ancient pines lean together at the crest as a natural
gate. Crossing the crest reveals the far side of the ridge: a level shoulder, a sea of cloud, terraced fields and the small shrine at the edge.

**Burning view:** the visitor's candle stands in a free-standing pierced stone lamp shrine at the terrace edge, close to camera, with the valley
and slow cloud visible beyond it.

**Endings:** burnt down, the candle melts into the old wax. Put out, a shortened candle remains with a thread of smoke.

**Sound:** wind in the pines, running spring water, insects and a far temple bell.

## Copy

The presence text counts people online and never says the visitor's lamp is the only light here.

## Gameplay logic

Every step must make sense inside the world.

| Step | Snow Roof Court | Pine Gate Steps |
|---|---|---|
| Season | Deep winter | Late spring, with water standing in the terraces |
| Where the lamp comes from | Gauze lanterns and oil cups wait on a table in the hall for visitors | Plain candles wait in a basket beside the shrine |
| Choosing the burn time | A larger lantern holds more oil | A larger candle holds more wax |
| Drawing a lamp | Shaping the lantern frame | Shaping the candle |
| The wish | Written on a wooden tag tied to the lantern | Written on a red cloth strip tied under the candle stand |
| Lighting | A taper takes flame from the altar lamp and lights the lantern | A taper takes flame from the shrine's oil lamp and lights the candle |
| Placing | The lantern is raised on the free cord | The candle stands at the centre of the stone lamp shrine |
| Leaving the page | A gust through the half-open door puts it out | A mountain gust puts it out |
| Lighting another | The new lamp takes the next free cord | The new candle takes the next free place |

**Quiet presence:** the visitor arrives in a quiet moment. People are heard but never seen: a morning bell, a distant axe, kitchen sounds at
Snow Roof; roosters, a far ox bell, frogs and evening voices from the village at Pine Gate.

**Weather:** Snow Roof has clear skies with fine snow blown from roofs and trees, so the peak stays visible. Pine Gate's cloud is thickest at dawn
and dusk and thinner at midday.

**Lamps in view:** ordinary lamps in the burning view stay lit at all times. Lamps that come on at dusk are only ever out of view while they change.

## Home

The home page is a wide mountain panorama at the player's local time of day. The player writes a wish here, then chooses where to carry it. Cloud Cliff
Court is no longer the home scene and may return later as a third place.

## Returning

A lamp the player has finished stays at that place. On a later visit the player can find their own old lamp among the others, with its shape, colour and
remaining wax. Only that player sees it.
